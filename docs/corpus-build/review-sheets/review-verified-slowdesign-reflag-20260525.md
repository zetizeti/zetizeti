## The standard being applied

From the file's own header (`slow-design.md` line 4) and the 23 May de-verdicting record
(`verification-slowdesign-critical.md` line 48), a slow-design lens entry is two-sided only when:
1. the critical claim is **attributed**, not asserted in the tool's own voice;
2. the conventional pole carries **argued** weight (the file's sibling entries do this with a
   `(The counter holds, and genuinely: …)` parenthetical) — *named is not enough*;
3. the questions handed to the learner **admit either answer** — no loaded pole, no preferred verdict.

All three flags below are failures of (2) or (3). None is a citation problem.

## Entry 1 — `who-slowness-excludes` (Pass 2)

**Flag:** Q2 de-gotcha. The body's counter *is* argued (the `(The counter holds: relentless
frictionlessness has its own harms…)` clause is present), so this is a question-only fix.

- **Q2 now:** *"Is the friction protecting the user, or flattering the people who already get it?"*
- **Problem:** "flattering the people who already get it" presupposes a vanity motive and a clique —
  the second pole is pejorative, so the question steers to the exclusion verdict.
- **Proposed:** *"Is the friction here serving the user, or mostly serving those already fluent in
  it?"*
- **Why it's de-gotcha'd:** both poles are now legitimate answers — friction can genuinely protect
  the user, *or* it can mainly reward the initiated — without the loaded "flattering". Keeps the
  exclusion question alive; removes the steer.

## Entry 2 — `visibility-and-the-unrewarded` (Pass 2)

**Flag:** Q2 de-gotcha. The body is strongly two-sided (it explicitly challenges *both* the
gamify-everything reflex *and* the we-stripped-all-metrics-so-we're-virtuous reflex), so again a
question-only fix.

- **Q2 now:** *"Is 'we don't gamify' a principle here, or a reason no one stays?"*
- **Problem:** "a reason no one stays" is catastrophising — it frames the anti-gamification stance as
  self-defeating, steering to "your principle is killing participation".
- **Proposed:** *"Is 'we don't gamify' sustaining participation here, or resting on a devotion you
  can't count on?"*
- **Why it's de-gotcha'd:** the substance is preserved (does the no-metrics stance actually sustain
  people, or quietly depend on an already-devoted few?) but both answers are now respectable; the
  doom-pole is gone.

## Entry 3 — `wandering-or-abandonment` — ✅ FIXED + re-pass-confirmed (25 May 2026)

> **Resolved.** Prayas chose "fix". Q2 rewritten to "…become being-lost — and which is this design
> closer to?" (de-gotcha'd) and an argued counter-pole added to the body. Independent re-pass confirmed
> the flag resolved, no new defect; entry remains `verified`. (Entries 1 & 2 — `who-slowness-excludes`,
> `visibility-and-the-unrewarded` — were NOT re-flagged by the independent upgrade-run Pass 2, so they
> default to keep-as-is.) Original flag detail retained below.

### (original flag — Pass 2 **and** Pass 3 — the structural one)

**Flag:** two defects. (a) the counter-pole is **named, not argued** — the body's second paragraph
closes on "…'we let users explore' can be a virtue or an alibi for not having designed orientation at
all", landing on *alibi* with no `(The counter holds…)` clause giving open-endedness genuine weight
(every two-sided sibling in the file has one). (b) Q2 is a gotcha binary.

**(a) Body — add an argued counter (proposed).** Append to the second paragraph, matching the
sibling pattern:

> *(The counter holds, and genuinely: some discoveries are only reachable by getting lost —
> over-orientation can sand away the very serendipity that was the point, and a confident wanderer
> rarely thanks you for the handrail.)*

This gives the open-ended pole an argued case (serendipity has a real cost-of-orientation), so the
entry stops leaning on "alibi" as the resting verdict.

**(b) Q2 — de-gotcha (proposed).**
- **Q2 now:** *"Is this open-endedness invitation, or abdication?"*
- **Problem:** "abdication" is the loaded pejorative; the binary steers to "open-endedness =
  laziness/failure to design".
- **Proposed:** *"Did you design this open-endedness, or arrive at it by not deciding?"*
- **Why it's de-gotcha'd:** both poles are legitimate — deliberate, designed openness *vs.*
  unconsidered absence — without moralising either as virtue or dereliction. (Q1, *"What tells your
  user there is something worth finding, before they find it?"*, is already a strong open question and
  needs no change.)
