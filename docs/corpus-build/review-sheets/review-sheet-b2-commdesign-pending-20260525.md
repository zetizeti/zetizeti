# GROUP 1 — Type & reading (4)

## 1. legibility-vs-readability
- **Sources:** Tinker, *Legibility of Print* (Iowa State University Press, 1963); Beier, *Reading Letters: Designing for Legibility* (BIS Publishers, 2012); Lupton, *Thinking with Type* (Princeton Architectural Press, 2004).
- **Tension:** Legibility (can a letter be told apart) vs readability (can a passage be read with ease and stamina) — the two don't always pull the same way, and the "most legible font" is weaker and more individual than dogma claims.
- **Questions:** Is this set to be told apart letter-by-letter, or to be read for pages — and which are you optimising? · Legible or readable for whom, and how would you know?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · C ✓ (20 papers — font choice significantly affects reading *speed* and individual fit, yet serif vs sans largely interchangeable when x-height is equalised; comprehension typically unaffected) · 4P-clean.
- **Outstanding:** none beyond the 20-clean spot-check sample. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

## 2. the-crystal-goblet ⚑ 4P-fixed
- **Sources:** Warde, "The Crystal Goblet, or Printing Should Be Invisible" (1930; collected 1955, Sylvan Press); Carson & Blackwell, *The End of Print* (Laurence King, 1995); Poynor, *No More Rules* (Yale University Press, 2003).
- **Tension:** Typography should efface itself so content arrives untouched (Warde) vs form is part of the meaning — type has a voice, expression can *be* the message (Carson; the postmodern turn, Poynor).
- **Questions:** Does this piece need its form to disappear, or to speak — and which are you giving it? · When you make it expressive, is the expression carrying the meaning or covering it?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory · 4P-fixed — **Pass 1 flag:** wording tracked Warde's protected wine-glass conceit + the "invisible" of her subtitle → **fixed:** re-expressed as self-effacement, the goblet image named as hers and consigned to the curtain (kept out of the question — invariant #4).
- **Outstanding:** confirmatory re-pass that the re-expression stays clear of the protected conceit. **Sign-off ☐**

## 3. the-typeface-has-a-voice
- **Sources:** Shaikh, Chaparro & Fox, "Perception of Fonts: Perceived Personality Traits and Uses" (2006); Hyndman, *Why Fonts Matter* (Virgin Books, 2016).
- **Tension:** A typeface sets a register before a word is read (readers reliably attribute personality; incongruence slows/colours interpretation) vs that "voice" is not universal — weaker in some languages, varies by reader/context, and intuition sometimes serves as well as data.
- **Questions:** Before a word is read, what is this typeface already saying — and did you choose that, or inherit it? · Whose ear are you tuning this voice for, and would it sound the same to a different reader?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · C ✓ (20 papers — congruence affects processing and appropriateness; counter preserved — effect weaker in a genderless language, context-varying, intuition sometimes sufficient) · 4P-clean.
- **Outstanding:** none beyond the spot-check sample. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

## 4. type-as-image-vs-text
- **Sources:** Drucker, *The Visible Word: Experimental Typography and Modern Art, 1909–1923* (University of Chicago Press, 1994).
- **Tension:** Type as transparent notation that disappears into meaning vs type as image/object/texture to be *seen* — the strongest work lives in the spectrum between, the risk is sliding to a pole unawares.
- **Questions:** Is this meant to be read, seen, or both — and where exactly on that line is it sitting? · At what point does your letterform stop being a word and become an image, and did you intend to cross it?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory · 4P-clean.
- **Outstanding:** none beyond the spot-check sample. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

# GROUP 2 — Rules, the grid & the system (5)

## 5. the-rule-and-the-recantation ⚑ 4P-fixed
- **Sources:** Tschichold, *Die neue Typographie* (Berlin, 1928; Eng. trans. Ruari McLean, *The New Typography*, University of California Press, 1995).
- **Tension:** Typographic rules as discovered truths vs historical conventions dressed as truths — the same practitioner (Tschichold) authored both the modernist manifesto and its post-war repudiation; the real move is knowing why a rule exists, hence when it is load-bearing.
- **Questions:** What is this rule actually holding up — and what happens to the work if you remove it? · Are you keeping this convention because it serves the piece, or because someone told you to?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory · 4P-fixed — **Pass 1 flag:** `the_tension`'s strength disagreed with the `framing_confidence` hedge on the "authoritarian" charge → **fixed:** the charge is grounded in Tschichold's *own* post-war words; framing_confidence reconciled ("renounced much of it" = partial return to classical book typography, not total disowning).

# GROUP 3 — Composition & perception (4)

## 10. gestalt-grouping-vs-disruption
- **Sources:** Wertheimer, "Laws of Organization in Perceptual Forms" (*Psychologische Forschung*, 1923); Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception* (University of California Press, 1954).
- **Tension:** The eye groups by proximity/similarity whether you intend it or not (Gestalt) — so you can work *with* grouping for instant legibility, or *against* it to create emphasis/friction; the question is whether a grouping is designed or accidental.
- **Questions:** Is this grouping one you intended, or one the eye made for you? · When you break the pattern, what does the break buy — emphasis, or confusion?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory (the grouping principles aren't in dispute; the *use-vs-disrupt* framing is the craft tension) · 4P-clean.

- **Questions:** What is this empty space doing — holding attention, or just unfilled? · If you filled it, would you be adding meaning, or only adding stuff?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory · 4P-clean — **Pass 2 advisory (not a failure):** carries its counter via *student errors* rather than two named positions — "softest-but-sound". Worth your eye, not a blocker.

- **Questions:** What does this message gain — and lose — if you strip it back? · Is your minimalism essential, or just the safe-looking choice?

## 13. colour-is-never-seen-alone ⚑ 4P-fixed

# GROUP 4 — Image, sign & meaning (5)

## 14. denotation-and-connotation
- **Sources:** Barthes, "Rhetoric of the Image," in *Image-Music-Text* (trans. Heath, Fontana, 1977; orig. *Communications* 4, 1964).
- **Tension:** An image carries denotation (what it literally shows) *and* connotation (learned cultural association that feels natural but is ideological) — the designer composes denotation but connotation completes in the viewer, out of a culture they don't control.
- **Questions:** Beneath what this image literally shows, what is it connoting — and to whom? · How much of that second meaning did you author, and how much did the culture?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory · 4P-clean — **Pass 2 advisory:** counter carried via student errors rather than two named positions — "softest-but-sound", not a blocker.

## 15. the-photograph-evidence-or-construction
- **Sources:** Sontag, *On Photography* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 1977); Barthes, *Camera Lucida* (trans. Howard, Hill and Wang, 1981; orig. *La Chambre claire*, Seuil, 1980).
- **Tension:** A photograph bears a trace of the real and reads as evidence vs every photograph being a construction — framed, timed, lit, captioned — an interpretation wearing the costume of evidence.
- **Questions:** What is this photograph being asked to prove, and how much of its truth is the trace versus the framing? · Are you using the image's air of objectivity honestly, or borrowing it?

## 16. universal-pictograms-vs-cultural
- **Sources:** Neurath, *International Picture Language* (Kegan Paul, 1936); Aicher & Krampen, *Zeichensysteme der visuellen Kommunikation* (Verlagsanstalt Alexander Koch, 1977) — the systematic basis of the 1972 Munich Olympic system.
- **Tension:** The dream of a visual language that crosses languages/literacies (Isotype; Aicher) — good systematised pictograms do travel vs a picture is still read through culture, and "intuitive" usually means "intuitive to people like the designer".
- **Questions:** What does this pictogram assume its viewer already knows — and is that knowledge really universal? · Where is the line between a symbol that crosses cultures and one that only crosses yours?

## 17. colour-meaning-universal-or-cultural
- **Sources:** Jonauskaitė et al., "Universal Patterns in Color-Emotion Associations Are Further Shaped by Linguistic and Geographic Proximity" (*Psychological Science*, 2020); Jonauskaitė & Mohr, "Do we feel colours? …" (*Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 2025).
- **Tension:** Colour-emotion associations are strikingly *universal* (shared across dozens of nations) *and* culture-specific at once (nation predicts beyond the universal pattern) — neither arbitrary nor fixed.
- **Questions:** Which part of this colour's meaning is broadly shared, and which is local to a culture? · Whose eyes is this palette tuned for — and would it read the same to someone else's?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (Pass 4 corrected an earlier "et al." → "& Mohr" on the 2025 review) · C ✓ (19 papers — ~.88 cross-national similarity in a 30-nation study, yet nation predicts beyond it; *the tension is the finding*) · 4P-clean.
- **Outstanding:** none beyond the spot-check sample. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

## 18. transmission-or-meaning-made ⚑ 4P-fixed (flagged by BOTH Pass 2 and Pass 3 — the clearest self-resolving tension in the file)
- **Sources:** Shannon & Weaver, *The Mathematical Theory of Communication* (University of Illinois Press, 1949); Hall, "Encoding/Decoding," in *Culture, Media, Language* (Hutchinson, 1980; from the 1973 CCCS stencilled paper).

- **Questions:** Is the difficulty here that the signal isn't arriving cleanly, or that the audience is making a meaning you didn't intend? · Which of the two models does this problem belong to — and what does that ask you to change?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (incl. Hall 1973/1980 dating) · theory · 4P-fixed — **Pass 2 + Pass 3 flags:** verdict-smuggling ("the weaker one") collapsing the tension toward Hall, a prescriptive close ("design for the reading, not just the sending"), and leading questions → **fixed:** the two models now stand as answering different questions, resolution left to the learner.
- **Outstanding:** confirmatory re-pass that no residual tilt toward Hall remains and the close stays non-prescriptive. **Sign-off ☐**

# GROUP 5 — Data & the diagram (2)

## 19. graphical-integrity-the-lie-factor
- **Sources:** Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (Graphics Press, 1983).
- **Tension:** Graphical integrity — keep the "lie factor" (effect-shown ÷ effect-in-data) near one; truncated axes/distorted proportions mislead (Tufte) vs emphasis being a legitimate part of communication, context often mattering more than the axis, some "distorted" framings genuinely serving a task.
- **Questions:** Is this choice making a real signal easier to see, or making a small effect look like a big one? · If a careful reader noticed what you did to this scale, would they feel informed or tricked?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (a *different* thesis from `clarity-vs-decoration`, which cites the same book for data-ink) · C ✓ (19 papers — y-axis truncation persistently misleads even after training; counter preserved — context can dominate, guideline-violations aren't the main real-world deception, some "deceptive" framings serve a task) · 4P-clean (Pass 3 confirmed its dedicated over-correction failure_mode).

## 20. the-diagram-and-what-it-discards
- **Sources:** Tufte, *Envisioning Information* (Graphics Press, 1990).

- **Status:** P4 ✓ (1990 first ed.) · theory · 4P-clean — **Pass 2 advisory:** counter via student errors rather than two named positions — "softest-but-sound", not a blocker.

# GROUP 6 — Authorship, voice, taste & time (5)

## 21. the-designer-as-author
- **Sources:** Rock, "The Designer as Author" (*Eye*, no. 20, vol. 5, 1996).
- **Tension:** The designer as neutral form-giver who makes someone else's content legible/attractive vs the designer as author with content, position, voice — Rock complicates how slippery "author" becomes when the medium is other people's messages.
- **Questions:** In this project, where does the authorship actually lie — with you, the client, or unclaimed? · If you call yourself the author here, what are you taking responsibility for; if not, who is?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (venue/year verified) · theory · 4P-clean (distinct from `author-vs-service-provider` in entrepreneurship-lens — that treats the business/pricing dimension; this treats authorial voice in the work).

## 22. the-mark-timeless-or-expressive
- **Sources:** Rand, *Thoughts on Design* (Wittenborn and Company, 1947).
- **Tension:** An effective mark is reductive/distinctive — simple enough to endure, earning meaning by association over time (Rand) vs maximal simplicity being generic/mute, while an expressive/illustrative mark communicates now at the risk of dating.
- **Questions:** What does this mark have to survive — fashion, scale, decades — and does its simplicity buy that, or just blandness? · Is the mark earning meaning over time, or trying to say everything at once?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (1947 first ed., Wittenborn) · theory · 4P-clean.
- **Outstanding:** none beyond the spot-check sample. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

## 23. ornament-crime-and-its-shadow
- **Sources:** Loos, "Ornament and Crime" (lecture 1910; first published *Les Cahiers d'aujourd'hui*, 1913); Canales & Herscher, "Criminal Skins…" (*Architectural History* 48, 2005).
- **Tension:** Removal of ornament as a mark of progress; "the plain version looks more sophisticated" (Loos) vs the essay's racialised shadow — Loos tied ornament to the "primitive", devaluing non-Western decorative cultures (Canales & Herscher); the reflex is not neutral taste, it has a history.
- **Questions:** Is this ornament empty, or is it carrying meaning, craft, or cultural memory? · Where does your sense that "plain is more sophisticated" actually come from?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (incl. corrected 1910/1913 dating + the Canales & Herscher article) · theory · 4P-clean — aligns with the standing instruction to reveal inherited reflexes *with* their critique, never innocently (cf. [[feedback-jugaad-reveal-with-critique]] in spirit).
- **Outstanding:** none beyond the spot-check sample. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

## 24. the-trained-eye-vs-the-vernacular
- **Sources:** Venturi, Scott Brown & Izenour, *Learning from Las Vegas* (MIT Press, 1972).
- **Tension:** Training that sharpens your eye also installs taste-reflexes that mark things "amateur"/"kitsch" — and the vernacular can communicate powerfully *because* it ignores those rules (Venturi et al.) vs the vernacular sometimes being genuinely confused/careless; don't romanticise it either.
- **Questions:** What is this "untrained" piece doing that actually works — and would your correction add clarity or just impose a taste? · Whose taste is your eye applying when it calls this amateur?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory · 4P-clean (resonates with the grassroots-innovation orientation — keep the vernacular-pole weighted, not patronised).
- **Outstanding:** none beyond the spot-check sample. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

## 25. the-word-in-time
- **Sources:** Brownie, *Transforming Type: New Directions in Kinetic Typography* (Bloomsbury Academic, 2015).
- **Tension:** Moving type gains time — pace, rhythm, transformation, emphasis can carry meaning (Brownie) vs motion competing directly with reading (a viewer can't re-read a word that has left the screen); animating for impact often spends legibility unnoticed.
- **Questions:** What is the motion carrying that stillness could not — or is it decoration over words that just needed reading? · Does the pace give a reader the time they actually need to read this?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (2015, Bloomsbury) · theory · 4P-clean (kept within communication design — type and meaning — distinct from the editing tension in `moving-image.md`).
