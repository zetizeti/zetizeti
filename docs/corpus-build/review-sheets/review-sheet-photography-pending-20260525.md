# THE NATURE OF THE PHOTOGRAPH (6)

## 1. the-index-and-the-trace · communication-design
- **Sources:** Bazin, "The Ontology of the Photographic Image" (1945; *What Is Cinema?* Vol. 1, trans. Gray, 1967); Krauss, "Notes on the Index", *October* 3 & 4 (1977).
- **Tension:** the photograph as causal *trace* (Bazin's automatism; Krauss's Peircean index) — proof that light touched the real vs the trace certifies only *that* something was there, not what it *means* (framed/timed/lit/captioned = authored).
- **Questions:** What does the causal trace in this image actually secure — and what does it only appear to? · If light genuinely touched this scene, does that make what the photograph *says* about it true?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓. Distinct from comm-design `the-photograph-evidence-or-construction` (this is the precise causal-index cut).
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 2. that-has-been · communication-design
- **Sources:** Barthes, *Camera Lucida* (Hill and Wang, 1981; orig. Cahiers du Cinéma/Gallimard/Le Seuil, 1980) — the noeme.
- **Tension:** the photograph certifies "that-has-been" (real past presence; time/death) vs the certainty is fragile/cultural — synthetic images sever it while keeping its look.
- **Questions:** Is this moving the viewer through what it shows, or through the fact that it *was* — real and now past? · Does the "that really happened" certainty still hold here, or only its look?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (French publisher corrected to the three-way co-edition) · 3P ✓.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 3. studium-and-punctum · communication-design
- **Sources:** Barthes, *Camera Lucida* (1980/1981).
- **Tension:** *studium* (coded, communicable interest you can author) vs *punctum* (the accidental detail that pricks a particular viewer — un-designable, so chasing it abandons what you can shape).
- **Questions:** What is the coded interest you can author here — and what is the accidental detail you can only hope for? · Are you relying on something that pricks a particular viewer unbidden, or something you can aim at everyone?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 4. aura-and-reproduction · communication-design
- **Sources:** Benjamin, "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction" (1935; *Illuminations*, 1968).
- **Tension:** reproduction withers *aura* (loss of the unique here-and-now) vs the same loss is an emancipation (mass reach), and the art market/digital edition *re-manufacture* aura.
- **Questions:** What does this image gain, and lose, by being endlessly reproducible? · Where an "original" or "aura" is claimed, is it real — or manufactured to restore what reproduction removed?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓ (Benjamin's own duality preserved). Not in comm-design.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 5. photographic-seeing · communication-design
- **Sources:** Szarkowski, *The Photographer's Eye* (MoMA, 1966).
- **Tension:** photography as its own way of seeing (the thing itself/detail/frame/time/vantage) you must anticipate vs "photographic seeing" as a formalist trap that prizes how the medium transforms over what the subject means.
- **Questions:** How will the camera transform what's in front of it — and are you anticipating that or ignoring it? · Are you attending to the subject, or to how photographically it reads — and which does this need?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓ — *fixed:* "frame" removed from vocab/felt-as to de-overlap with #7 (Pass 2).
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 6. is-photography-art · communication-design
- **Sources:** Baudelaire, "The Modern Public and Photography" ("Salon of 1859").
- **Tension:** photography as mechanical record (Baudelaire's 1859 charge) vs selection/framing/timing/vision as where the art lies — reborn as the live question "what in this image is *mine*?"
- **Questions:** What makes this a considered photograph rather than a mere record — and does that line still mean anything to you? · If a machine did much of the seeing here, where is your vision in it?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓ — *fixed:* "refuge of failed painters" recast (read as a quotation; Pass 1); "now-settled answer" → "outlasts that historical quarrel" (Pass 3); Q1 re-anchored on art-status to de-overlap with #33 (Pass 2).

# TRUTH · DOCUMENT · THE DIGITAL (5)

## 7. the-document-and-its-frame · communication-design
- **Sources:** Szarkowski, *The Photographer's Eye* (1966) — "the frame".
- **Tension:** the frame *excludes*, and what's outside it can reverse the meaning (authorship's least visible act) vs all representation selects — a frame isn't a lie, and demanding the "whole picture" can discredit any image.
- **Questions:** What is just outside this frame — and would seeing it change what the photograph seems to say? · The edges were chosen; does that make this less true, or just authored?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓. Distinct from #5 (medium-seeing) and #23 (post-capture crop).
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 8. the-decisive-moment-or-the-constructed-image · communication-design
- **Sources:** Cartier-Bresson, *The Decisive Moment* (1952; orig. *Images à la sauvette*); Wall, "Marks of Indifference" (1995).
- **Tension:** the image *caught* at the crystallising instant vs the image *built* (staged tableau) that argues the decisive moment is a style.
- **Questions:** Does this draw its power from being caught at the right instant, or from being built? · Is it honest about which — and does "this really happened" depend on not knowing?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ ("decisive moment" framed as his idea/title, not coinage — de Retz epigraph) · 3P ✓.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 9. staging-the-documentary · communication-design
- **Sources:** Lange "Migrant Mother" (1936); Rothstein steer-skull (1936); RA→FSA (renamed 1937).
- **Tension:** shaping serves a *larger* truth (composition for a told truth) vs arranging forfeits the documentary authority of mere recording.
- **Questions:** Did the shaping clarify something real, or manufacture an impression that wasn't there? · Does the viewer need to know it was arranged for the image to be honest?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 10. the-caption-directs-the-photograph · communication-design
- **Sources:** Barthes, "Rhetoric of the Image" (1964; *Image-Music-Text*, 1977) — anchorage/relay.
- **Tension:** text *anchors* the polysemous photograph (same image, opposite captions) vs the strong image exceeds its caption, and over-anchoring manipulates.
- **Questions:** How much of this photograph's meaning is in the image, and how much in the words beside it? · If you changed the caption, would the picture still say what you intend — and what does that tell you about where the meaning lives?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓. Co-retrieves with comm-design `denotation-and-connotation` (same essay) — distinct (anchorage of the news photo vs denotation/connotation of the ad image); already vocabulary-separated.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 11. post-photography-and-evidence · communication-design
- **Sources:** Mitchell, *The Reconfigured Eye* (MIT Press, 1992).
- **Tension:** digital dissolves the indexical guarantee (seeing ≠ believing) vs the photograph's authority was *always* social (trust/provenance), and verification can rebuild it.
- **Questions:** What does this image's claim to be believed rest on now — the pixels, or the provenance around them? · If it could have been generated without trace, what would still make it trustworthy?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓ — *fixed:* generic evidence tokens trimmed from vocab to de-overlap with comm-design's evidence/construction entry (Pass 2). Distinct (digital-era dissolution).
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

# AESTHETICS · FORM · TECHNIQUE (7)

## 21. black-and-white-or-colour · communication-design
- **Sources:** *William Eggleston's Guide* (MoMA, 1976, essay by Szarkowski).
- **Tension:** B&W as "serious" (abstracts, dignifies, directs to form) vs colour as truer/meaningful (the 1976 Eggleston show overturned the prejudice) — each adds and throws away.
- **Questions:** What does converting this to B&W add — and what real information does it throw away? · Are you choosing monochrome (or colour) for what it does to *this* image, or by reflex about what each "means"?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 22. sharpness-or-blur · communication-design
- **Sources:** Group f/64 (1932; Weston, Adams, Cunningham) vs Pictorialism.
- **Tension:** sharpness as honesty/the thing fully seen vs blur/softness as expressive resource — and the cult of sharpness as a fetish for resolution over meaning.
- **Questions:** What is sharpness doing here — rendering precisely, or standing in for a quality the image doesn't need? · If part of this were soft, would it lose information or gain expression?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 23. the-frame-and-the-crop · communication-design
- **Sources:** Cartier-Bresson's no-crop / full-negative discipline (*The Decisive Moment*, 1952).

- **Questions:** Does composing in-camera or cropping afterward serve this picture better — and why? · Is the crop you're reaching for rescuing the image, or covering for not having seen it clearly?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · 3P ✓. Distinct from #7 (documentary exclusion at capture).
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready. Sign-off ☐**

## 24. visualisation-or-discovery · communication-design

## 35. the-nude-as-form-or-object · communication-design
