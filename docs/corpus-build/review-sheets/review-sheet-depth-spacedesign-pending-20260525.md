## 1. conserve-as-found-or-restore-to-an-ideal — ✅ clean on all four passes
- **Sources:** Ruskin, *The Seven Lamps of Architecture* (Smith, Elder & Co., 1849), "The Lamp of Memory"; Viollet-le-Duc, "Restauration", in *Dictionnaire raisonné…* (A. Morel, 1854–1868).
- **Tension:** conserve the authentic-but-broken *as found* (Ruskin — you cannot reproduce what time did) vs restore toward a coherent recovered ideal (Viollet-le-Duc — "a condition of completeness which may never have existed").
- **Questions:** Where does this thing's value sit — in the authentic marks of its age, or in a coherent whole you could recover? · If you complete what time damaged, what real history goes with it — and if you leave it as found, who can no longer read or use it?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (Viollet quote verified a faithful standard translation) · theory · 4P clean.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready.**  **Sign-off ☐**

## 2. the-plan-from-above-or-the-street-from-below — flagged-then-fixed (Pass 2)
- **Sources:** Le Corbusier, *The City of To-morrow…* (John Rodker, 1929; ← *Urbanisme*, 1924); Jacobs, *Death and Life of Great American Cities* (Random House, 1961); Alexander, Ishikawa & Silverstein, *A Pattern Language* (OUP, 1977); Caro, *The Power Broker* (Knopf, 1974).
- **Tension:** comprehensive expert-led planning (Le Corbusier / Moses) vs bottom-up incremental urbanism (Jacobs / Alexander) — order authored whole vs grown street by street.
- **Questions:** What in this actually needs to be ordered whole from above, and what would be better left to grow street by street? · If you plan it all at once, whose life and improvisation does the plan foreclose — and if you don't, who coordinates what no single actor will?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · theory · 4P — *fixed:* the top-down pole's affirmative case (water/sanitation/transit/coordination) moved into the **body**, not just the balance clause; Moses reframed so Caro's indictment is **attributed** ("documents and indicts") rather than riding silently on the citation.
- **Outstanding:** none — the top-down pole now carries argued body weight. **Ready** (your eye on whether the Le Corbusier/Moses pole-pairing reads fairly). **Sign-off ☐**

## 3. the-perfected-type-or-the-appropriable-frame — flagged-then-fixed (Pass 1 BLOCKER)
- **Sources:** Le Corbusier, *Towards a New Architecture* (Etchells trans., John Rodker, 1927); the *Existenzminimum* programme of the 1929 CIAM Frankfurt congress (Ernst May et al.; see Mumford, *The CIAM Discourse on Urbanism*, MIT Press, 2000); Habraken, *Supports: An Alternative to Mass Housing* (Architectural Press, 1972; ← *De dragers en de mensen*, 1961).
- **Tension:** the optimised standard dwelling repeated (Le Corbusier / Existenzminimum) vs the durable *support* + inhabitant-determined infill (Habraken, "open building") — finished-and-optimised vs unfinished-and-appropriable.
- **Questions:** Is this better as one optimised solution repeated, or as a frame the inhabitants finish themselves — and which serves the people who'll live in it here? · What does an open frame let people decide that a finished unit can't — and what does leaving it unfinished fail to give those who lack the means to complete it?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ (Le Corbusier + Habraken confirmed) · theory · 4P — **fixed (blocker):** *Existenzminimum* was wrongly bound to Le Corbusier with no source → decoupled (it is a CIAM/Frankfurt-1929 / Ernst May programme) and the Mumford (2000) source added; asymmetric Q2 rebalanced to interrogate the open-frame pole; "flexibility" failure-mode token varied (FTS distinctness from `the-building-after-the-architect`).

## 4. the-compact-city-or-the-room-to-sprawl — flagged-then-fixed (Pass 2, minor)
- **Sources:** Newman & Kenworthy, *Cities and Automobile Dependence* (Gower, 1989); Bruegmann, *Sprawl: A Compact History* (Univ. of Chicago Press, 2005).
- **Tension:** compact dense mixed-use form as the responsible/sustainable choice (Newman & Kenworthy) vs dispersal answering genuine preferences and the case against sprawl being partly aesthetic/moral (Bruegmann) — held non-linear: "denser is greener" reverses at the extremes.
- **Questions:** What does density — and what does spreading out — actually buy and cost at this scale and place, and for whom, rather than treating either as a general virtue? · If you build it compact, where might the gains reverse (cost, crowding, local emissions) — and if you let it spread, what gets locked in?
- **Status:** P4 ✓ · **C ✓** (20 papers — genuinely two-sided & non-linear: Gudipudi ≥42% / Güneralp PNAS pro-compact, vs Xu/Yin/Xing/Muñiz/Zarco-Periñán contesting/reversing) · 4P — *fixed:* Q1 + close made symmetric across both poles. Pass 3 confirmed it holds without tilt.
- **Outstanding:** none. **Ready** (strongest of the empirical pair). **Sign-off ☐**

## 5. defensible-space-or-the-open-street — flagged-then-fixed (Pass 2+3 BLOCKER) · *slug renamed*
- **Sources:** Oscar Newman, *Defensible Space* (Macmillan, 1972); Mike Davis, *City of Quartz* (Verso, 1990, "Fortress L.A."); Setha Low, *Behind the Gates* (Routledge, 2003).
- **Tension:** suppress crime by design — territoriality, controlled access, natural surveillance (Newman / CPTED) vs the fortress-urbanism critique that the same logic excludes, hardens against the marginalised, and moves crime rather than reducing it (Davis; Low).
- **Questions:** Does this safety move protect the people who use the space, or mainly control who is allowed to belong here — and who decides which? · Is it reducing actual danger here, or mainly moving crime elsewhere and changing how safe the space feels?
