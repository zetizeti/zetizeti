## entry: yes-and-or-the-plan
**discipline:** theatre-and-performance
**vocabulary:** improvise, improvisation, improv, devise, devising, rehearsal, script, scene, plan, spontaneous, yes and, workshop, actors, play, structure
**felt as:** should we devise it or write it first; let's just improvise and see; i need a script before rehearsals; the improv was great but it doesn't hold together; yes, and; i'm scared of losing control of it; it feels stiff when it's planned.
**the_tension:** Lewis and Lovatt (2013) found that people's scores on a standard idea task (how many, how original and how varied their ideas were) rose after verbal or musical improvisation but not after a control task; Felsman, Gunawardena and Seifert (2020) repeated the finding after twenty minutes of improv exercises, and found it also raised people's tolerance of uncertainty. Improvisation, on this evidence, loosens set patterns of thinking. The counter is that these are measurements taken right after short exercises on laboratory tasks, and a play also needs what improvisation does not supply: shape, cuts, an ending that has been chosen. Many makers devise and then write, or write and then open the script to the actors. The maker's move is to ask which part of this piece needs loosening and which part needs deciding, and who in the process does each.
**failure_modes:** improvising endlessly and never choosing; fixing the script so early that the actors' discoveries have nowhere to go; treating a good rehearsal-room moment as a structure.
**questions_it_invites:** Which part of this piece do you want to discover in rehearsal, and which do you need to decide before anyone arrives? · What happened in an improvisation that you would not have written?
**sources:** Lewis & Lovatt, "Breaking away from set patterns of thinking: Improvisation and divergent thinking," *Thinking Skills and Creativity* 9, 46–58 (2013), doi:10.1016/j.tsc.2013.03.001 · Felsman, Gunawardena & Seifert, "Improv experience promotes divergent thinking, uncertainty tolerance, and affective well-being," *Thinking Skills and Creativity* 35, 100632 (2020), doi:10.1016/j.tsc.2020.100632.

## entry: inside-or-beside-the-role
**discipline:** theatre-and-performance
**vocabulary:** actor, role, character, perform, performer, empathy, inside, become, play, part, monologue, rehearse, distance, audience, stage
**felt as:** the actor has to become her; i want to write a part an actor can live inside; should the performer stay themselves; how much do i give the actor; the character is far from anyone in the cast; i'm writing a monologue; i want the audience to see the actor too.
**the_tension:** Goldstein and Winner (2012) followed children and adolescents through a year of acting classes or other arts training; those who acted gained in empathy, and the adolescents also improved in reading other people's thoughts and feelings. Acting, on this evidence, can exercise the capacity to step into another person's shoes, though the study followed young people over a year of classes and does not show the same for adults or for an audience, and does-the-story-make-them-kinder shows how contested such claims have been for reading. Gallagher and Gallagher (2020) add that, at certain points of preparation, an actor has to hold two things apart, the character and their own portrayal of it, and that the actor's understanding of the character, built through narrative and imagination, later integrates with more immediate empathy in performance; the audience, too, sees actor and character at once. A part can be written to make that doubleness more or less visible. The writer's move is to ask how much of the actor they want the audience to see through the character, and what the part gives the actor to understand rather than only to show.
**failure_modes:** writing a part so fully specified that the actor has nothing to understand for themselves; assuming the audience should forget the actor completely; writing a character from the outside that no performer could enter.
**questions_it_invites:** What does this part ask an actor to understand that the lines do not say? · How much of the actor do you want the audience to see through the character?
**sources:** Goldstein & Winner, "Enhancing Empathy and Theory of Mind," *Journal of Cognition and Development* 13(1), 19–37 (2012), doi:10.1080/15248372.2011.573514 · Gallagher & Gallagher, "Acting Oneself as Another: An Actor's Empathy for her Character," *Topoi* 39(4), 779–790 (2020; online 12 Jan 2019), doi:10.1007/s11245-018-9624-7.
**provenance:** pending
**framing_confidence:** high — both as stated in the abstracts (Gallagher & Gallagher's "twofoldness" after Wollheim).
