# Domain corpus: The Own-Purpose Lens

> **Part B — cross-cutting lens.** Synthetic, retrieved per turn via FTS5. Aimed at the learner's **conventional** reflex to reach for an external warrant — the method, the framework, the best practice, the reference project, what the brief says, what the field expects — in place of a reason of their own. Its vocabulary is keyed to those conventional words, so the lens activates exactly when a student reaches for them.
> 🔴 **THIS IS NOT ANOTHER METHOD CRITIQUE, and the corpus already holds eight of those.** `the-method-or-the-judgement` and `design-thinking-and-its-critics` already argue about whether process can substitute for judgement; `after-the-rules` (Poynor) and `the-rule-and-the-recantation` (Tschichold) hold the breaking of a discipline's rules; `the-designerly-leap-or-the-evidence` (Cross), `reflection-in-action-and-wicked-problems` (Schön, Rittel), `knowing-by-making` (Frayling) and `the-canonical-defended` hold the rest. **None of them asks the two questions this lens asks: when is your own sense admissible as evidence, and what is your reason for making this one** — the first is an epistemics question with real empirical conditions attached, and the second has no external source by construction.
> **Held two-sided, and this file needs it more than most.** "Trust yourself, not the textbook" is the flattering half of the thought and it is the half a student arrives already holding. Every entry below carries the counter at full strength, and `is-your-certainty-evidence` exists to make the flattering half answerable to something.

## entry: the-rule-you-are-meant-to-outgrow
**discipline:** own-purpose
**provenance:** pending
**vocabulary:** rule, principle, guideline, framework, method, process, methodology, best, practice, standard, correct, proper, textbook, taught, supposed, should
**felt as:** what's the correct process for this; i want to follow best practice; is this the right way to do it; the framework says you should; we were taught to do it this way; i'm supposed to do the research phase first; what's the standard approach.
**the_tension:** A rule is a scaffold handed to somebody who cannot yet see what matters in a situation. Where are you with this — do you still need the rule to see anything at all, or are you holding it because letting go of it would mean deciding?
Stuart Dreyfus, summarising the five-stage model developed with Hubert Dreyfus, describes a beginner being given context-free rules precisely because they cannot yet tell the relevant from the irrelevant, and describes the expert's repertoire of experienced situations becoming so large that the situation itself suggests the action without a rule being consulted. The sentence that matters for a student is his: **if one seeks the safety of rules, one will not get beyond competence** (*Bulletin of Science, Technology & Society*, 2004; earlier in *Mind Over Machine*, Free Press, 1986). The lens asks the student to locate themselves on that path rather than to take a position on rules in general. (The counter holds, and it is the half that gets dropped: **the rule stage is necessary and cannot be skipped.** Dreyfus is explicit that the beginner's job is to follow the rules and gain experience, and that instruction begins by decomposing the situation into features a beginner can actually recognise. A student who discards method before they have the experience to replace it with has not become an expert; they have become a novice with no scaffold, and the work shows it.)
**failure_modes:** holding on to a process past the point where it is doing any seeing for you, because following it feels like working; dropping method early, calling it intuition, and producing what a beginner without rules produces.
**questions_it_invites:** With this particular thing, can you already tell what matters without the framework telling you — or not yet? · What is the method deciding for you here that you would otherwise have to decide?
**sources:** Dreyfus, "The Five-Stage Model of Adult Skill Acquisition" (*Bulletin of Science, Technology & Society*, 2004); Dreyfus & Dreyfus, *Mind Over Machine* (Free Press, 1986); Dreyfus & Dreyfus, "Peripheral Vision: Expertise in Real World Contexts" (*Organization Studies*, 2005).
**framing_confidence:** medium-high — the model is a phenomenological account rather than an experimental finding, and the entry describes it as such rather than as measurement. ⚠️ Scholarly criticism of the model exists and was not retrieved in this pass; treat the "necessary stages" claim as the authors' position. ## entry: is-your-certainty-evidence
**discipline:** own-purpose
**provenance:** pending
**vocabulary:** feel, feels, sense, instinct, gut, intuition, know, sure, obvious, right, taste, judgement, confident, believe, convinced
**felt as:** it just feels right; i know this is the right direction; my gut says go with this one; it's obvious to me; i trust my instinct on this; i can't explain it but i'm sure; that's just my taste and i stand by it.
**the_tension:** You are sure. The question is not whether to respect that, it is what your certainty is evidence of. Have you had the kind of practice at this that could have taught you the pattern you think you are seeing — repeated attempts, in a situation that behaves consistently, where you found out afterwards whether you were right?
Kahneman and Klein, arriving from two traditions that usually disagree, set out the conditions under which professional intuition can be trusted: the environment must be regular enough to contain learnable patterns, and the person must have had an opportunity to learn those regularities, which means adequate and reasonably prompt feedback. Their sharpest conclusion is the uncomfortable one — **subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgement accuracy** (*American Psychologist*, 64(6), 2009, 515–526). Dane, Rockmann and Pratt found across two studies that intuition outperforms analysis only at a high level of domain expertise (*Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 2012). Much design work is the hard case: outcomes arrive late or never, they are confounded, and nobody tells you which of your choices did the work. (The counter holds, and genuinely: this cannot become a demand to justify every move in advance, which is not how anyone designs, and demanding it produces timid, defensible, dead work. Cross's point stands elsewhere in this corpus that proposing a solution and its frame together is a real form of reasoning. The question is not whether to act on judgement — you must — but whether to treat the feeling of certainty as though it were a finding.)
**failure_modes:** reading the strength of a conviction as evidence for its content; demanding advance justification for every move until nothing is proposed at all.
**questions_it_invites:** What have you done enough times, with an answer afterwards, to have learned this pattern from? · If you turned out to be wrong here, when and how would you find out?
**sources:** Kahneman & Klein, "Conditions for intuitive expertise: A failure to disagree" (*American Psychologist*, 64(6), 2009, 515–526); Dane, Rockmann & Pratt, "When should I trust my gut? Linking domain expertise to intuitive decision-making effectiveness" (*Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 2012).
**framing_confidence:** high — Kahneman & Klein is a joint position paper by two opposed camps, which is unusually strong ground, and the entry quotes its own conclusion rather than extending it. Framing PENDING three-pass + Prayas's sign-off.

## entry: the-part-you-cannot-write-down
**discipline:** own-purpose
**provenance:** pending
**vocabulary:** explain, justify, articulate, rationale, reason, document, write, defend, present, argue, evidence, prove, why, because
**felt as:** i need to be able to justify this in the crit; how do i explain why this works; i have to write up the rationale; they'll ask me to defend it; i can't articulate it so maybe it's wrong; i need a reason i can present.
**the_tension:** Being unable to say why something works is treated as a sign that it does not. Sometimes it is. Sometimes it is a sign that the thing you know is not the kind of thing that fits in a sentence. Which is it here — and what would you have to do to find out, other than trying harder to phrase it?
Michael Polanyi's claim is that skilled knowing exceeds what can be stated: we know more than we can tell (*The Tacit Dimension*, 1966; the fuller argument in *Personal Knowledge*, Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1958). A skilled performer attends *through* the details of what they are doing towards what they are doing it for, and turning attention back onto the details breaks the performance. This is why a method document can carry a discipline's procedures and not its competence, and why "write down how you did it" is not a complete transfer. (The counter holds and is serious: **what cannot be said also cannot be examined, taught, corrected or disagreed with.** Unstated judgement is exactly where bias, habit and a field's inherited prejudices sit undisturbed, and "you wouldn't understand, it's tacit" is available to anybody who has not thought it through. Being unable to explain is not evidence of depth. The demand to articulate is often the only thing standing between a practice and its own unexamined assumptions.)
**failure_modes:** dressing an unexamined preference as tacit knowledge, where nobody can reach it; discarding a sound move because it could not be justified in the vocabulary a crit happens to use.
**questions_it_invites:** Is this something you cannot yet say, or something that does not survive being said? · If you had to be wrong about this, where would the error be hiding — in the part you can explain, or the part you cannot?
**sources:** Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (1966); Polanyi, *Personal Knowledge: Towards a Post-Critical Philosophy* (Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1958).
**framing_confidence:** medium-high — a philosophical position, presented as one, with the counter given at full strength because the tacit-knowledge argument is unusually easy to misuse as a defence against scrutiny. Framing PENDING three-pass + Prayas's sign-off.

## entry: why-this-and-why-you
**discipline:** own-purpose
**provenance:** pending
**vocabulary:** brief, client, user, market, audience, need, requirement, asked, expected, industry, trend, relevant, useful, impact, because
**felt as:** the brief asks for this; users need it; there's a market for it; that's what the industry expects; it's a relevant problem right now; it would be genuinely useful; the client wants it this way.
**the_tension:** Every reason you have given for this project could be given by somebody else for the same project. That is what makes them good reasons and also what makes them interchangeable. What is your reason for making this one — the part that would not survive being handed to another person along with the brief?
Dreyfus's account of skill puts something specific where this question lands: he argues that moving past competence requires emotional investment in the choice, and that what matters is taking responsibility for one's own successful and unsuccessful choices rather than seeking the safety of rules (*Bulletin of Science, Technology & Society*, 2004). A reason that is entirely external — the brief, the market, the requirement — leaves nothing to take responsibility for, because nothing in the decision was yours. The lens is not asking for a manifesto. It is asking what, in this specific project, you would still want to be true if the brief changed. (The counter holds, and it is the sharper edge: **a purpose that cannot be questioned is not a purpose, it is a position.** "This is what I care about" is available as an answer to every criticism, and used that way it makes the work unaccountable to the people it is supposedly for — which is how a designer's preference gets served under the description of a vision. The calling literature also records real costs on the person: Duffy et al. name burnout, workaholism and exploitation among the outcomes of living a calling, alongside the satisfaction — *Journal of Counseling Psychology*, 2018.)
**failure_modes:** a project whose every justification is borrowed, so that no choice inside it is actually being made; a stated purpose that is immune to evidence, and functions as a refusal to hear anything.
**questions_it_invites:** What would you still want to be true about this if the brief changed tomorrow? · Which part of this decision is yours to be wrong about?
**sources:** Dreyfus, "The Five-Stage Model of Adult Skill Acquisition" (*Bulletin of Science, Technology & Society*, 2004); Duffy et al., "Work as a calling: A theoretical model" (*Journal of Counseling Psychology*, 2018).
**framing_confidence:** medium — the central move is a framing rather than an empirical claim, and the Dreyfus material is used for what it says about involvement and responsibility in skill acquisition, not stretched into a claim about purpose in general. ⚠️ The Duffy author list beyond the first name was not settled against Crossref and is written as "et al." for that reason.
