# Method note: questioning a text (Clean Language, re-pointed at an artefact)

> **Origin:** original prose, written for zetizeti, adapting the publicly-known Clean Language
> questioning *technique* (also credited in `clean-questioning.md`) to a found text rather than a
> speaking learner. The four **Clean Language Principles** are reproduced verbatim below under their
> **CC BY 4.0** licence, with attribution (Leaders in Clean, 2025); everything else is the technique
> *described*, not an author's expression reproduced. The felt sense (Gendlin) is described only.
> **Part of the critical register — behind the curtain.** Loaded only on the criticism surface, NOT
> resident in the Socratic prompt (`corpus/criticism/` is not globbed by `loadMethodCore`).
> **provenance:** pending — awaiting Prayas's Gate-4 sign-off (architecture.md §3a).

## Why a sibling note is needed

`clean-questioning.md` is written toward a learner narrating their *own* felt sense — "use *their*
word", "hand the situation back to *them*". Criticism turns the questioning toward an **artefact**:
a found text the student is pushing back on — an idea they want to pressure-test, whatever its source
(a conclusion they reached, an answer a machine gave them, a claim from anywhere). The Clean-Language *discipline* carries over unchanged —
question, do not answer; reuse exact words; advance the thought rather than loop — but the target
shifts from the learner's interior to a thing on the page. This note adapts the forms; it is the
hidden *how*, never what the student sees as the framing (that is the critical register's idiom).

## The principles this rests on (reproduced under CC BY 4.0)

Both modes hold a discipline the Clean Language Principles state almost exactly. Reproduced verbatim
under their licence — note how (b) and (d) already *are* the verdict-drift discipline:

> **Clean Language principles.** A person is 'being clean' when they:
> a. Preserve others' experience precisely as they express it (including metaphors and non-verbals); and
> b. Refrain from introducing concepts, metaphors, judgements, evaluations or assumptions; and
> c. Invite others to attend to their experience without intending to change it; and
> d. Only introduce words that do not suggest new content.
>
> — *Clean Language Principles © 2025 by Leaders in Clean (a core contributor group; with
> acknowledgement to David Grove). Licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).
> Source: https://cleanlanguage.com/clean-language-principles/*

Re-pointed at a found text: "preserve others' experience precisely" → *quote the text's own word, do
not paraphrase it*; "refrain from introducing … judgements, evaluations or assumptions" → the
never-deposit rule the verdict-drift guard enforces; "only introduce words that suggest no new content"
→ why a question reuses the text's word, never the questioner's.

## Whose words are reused now

In Socratic mode the exact words are the learner's. With a text there are two word-sources, and the
distinction matters: the **text's** words (the phrasing on the page) and the **student's** words
(how they speak about it). Quote the text's word when pointing at a spot — *"the text says
'seamless' here"* — so the object stays in view and is not paraphrased into the questioner's model of
it. Reuse the student's word when developing what *they* notice — *"you said this felt 'pushy' —
where, in the text, is the pushy?"*. Never silently merge the two; that would put the questioner's
reading where the student's should be.

## The forms, re-pointed at the page

The Clean forms from the method core become, for a text:

- locating in the artefact: *"And whereabouts in this does the [their word] happen?"*
- developing what is on the page: *"And what kind of [its word] is that [its word]?"*
- attending to the not-yet-said: *"And when you read it and something doesn't sit right but you can't
  name it yet — what's there?"* (Gendlin's felt sense, now about the student's response to the text)
- the criticism-specific opening (from the bridge): *"Is this describing, or deciding for you?"*

## The same restraints, with one added

Direction, continuity, genuine questions, problem-posing-not-banking — all hold exactly as in the
method core. One restraint is sharper here: with a text in front of them it is tempting for the tool
to start *reading the text for* the student — to narrate what the text "really" does. That is just
the deposited conclusion wearing an analyst's coat. The text is the student's to read; the tool asks,
points, and waits. The felt-sense questions are how a student is helped to find *their own* words for
what the text is doing — never supplied the words by the tool.
