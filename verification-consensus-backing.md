## ✅ friction-and-frictionlessness — CONFIRMED, tension preserved

Friction is *sometimes* beneficial, and the tension stays live:
- Microboundaries / designed friction disrupt mindless interaction, prompting reflection and reducing data-entry errors [F2].

## ✅ dark-patterns-and-ethics — CONFIRMED (strongly), tension preserved

## ◐ recognition-rather-than-recall — MECHANISM CONFIRMED, direct heuristic thinner

## ✅ affordances-and-signifiers — CONFIRMED, tension preserved

## ✅ modes-and-modelessness — CONFIRMED, tension preserved

- Monk 1986: realistic systems inevitably have modes; action-contingent sound signalling cut mode errors to **a third** [MO1]. Sellen, Kurtenbach & Buxton: visual and kinesthetic feedback reduce mode errors; **user-maintained** mode states beat **system-maintained** ones [MO5]. - **Tension preserved precisely:** mode keys *reduce panel complexity* (the economy) **but** cause "slower and more often incorrect usage," moderated by practice — fewer for occasional users, dedicated keys for experts [MO19].

## ✅ conceptual-and-mental-models — CONFIRMED, balanced framing vindicated

Both sides of the revised "where does responsibility sit" question hold:

## ✅ sketching-and-fidelity — CONFIRMED, the corpus's cleanest symmetric tension

Both poles carry demonstrated costs — the entry's mirror-image failure modes:
- **Low-fidelity** invites divergent feedback and broad exploration [SK1, SK7] *but* induces idealization/projection, a validity risk [SK2].

## ✅ ironies-of-automation — CONFIRMED (strongly), tension holds

- The original is confirmed [I3]. The **out-of-the-loop** problem is robust: operators are handicapped in taking over after automation failure, from skill *and* situation-awareness loss [I2]; removing the operator from implementation is detrimental to recovery when automation fails [I9]; reliance → complacency → often-unnoticed skill erosion [I5].
- **Extended directly to the tool's territory:** "Ironies of AI" [I15], "Ironies of Generative AI" — role shift from production to evaluation; automation "makes easy tasks easier and hard tasks harder" [I8]; and specifically **"Ironies of AI-Assisted *Design*"** — deskilling, cognitive offloading, misplaced responsibility among UX practitioners [I7].
- **Tension holds (not one-sided):** the value side is real but *conditional* — intermediate/adaptive levels of automation improve performance under *normal* conditions and can counter OOTL [I9, I11]; operators expect safety to rise "as long as it works as expected" [I20]. So automation *frees* in the normal case and *deskills/endangers* at the failure boundary — the entry's genuine two-sided framing.

## ✅ situated-action-vs-plans — CONFIRMED, and the field itself debated the tension

- Foundational source confirmed [SA-S] — hugely influential (≈7,000+ citations); Suchman recast plans as *resources for* action, not determinants, launching ethnographically-driven design that surfaced workplace improvisation [SA6].
- **The balanced edit is vindicated by the field's own pushback:** Bardram argues "situated planning" — plans are *made of* situated action and are essential to realising work, not opposed to it [SA7]; Ng argues (even on phenomenological grounds) it is "impossible to consider an action without an a priori plan," and plan-deviation falls as situations become non-problematic [SA12]; Law's experiment "refutes Suchman's deemphasis on the role of plans" [SA19]. So "hold a legible happy-path *and* graceful departure" is exactly where the literature settled.
- *Backing is theoretical/qualitative + a few experiments — flagged honestly.*

## ✅ embodied-interaction — CONFIRMED, balanced framing vindicated

- Embodied/tangible interaction frequently improves engagement, learning gains, and retention vs screen/tablet [EM4 (whole-body physics sim > desktop), EM9 (haptic force-feedback best for force concepts)]; museums adopt tangible/embodied interaction precisely to counter screens that "disengage visitors from materiality."
- **Counter pole is real (vindicates the edit):** embodied interaction does *not* always beat mouse-based [EM6]; "high interactivity can be overwhelming and may lower learning performance" [EM15]; effectiveness depends on **bodily-engagement × task-integration** [EM3] and is "limited by type of learning, age, or redundant strategy" [EM11]. So weighing the body against what a screen genuinely gains (accessibility, precision, scale) is empirically warranted.
- *Dourish [EM-D] is the foundational theory; the empirical contingency comes from the embodied-learning literature.*

## ✅ restraint-is-not-neutral — SUPPORTED (design-theory + partial empirics), tension preserved

- **Lineage, not neutrality:** flat/minimal design is explicitly traced to the Swiss style of the 1940s–50s [RN2] and Bauhaus geometry/grids [RN6]; semiotic/rhetorical analyses read flat design as ideology — a "marker of hypermodernism" deconstructing aesthetic hierarchy [RN10] and a persuasive *rhetorical* shift, not a neutral default [RN7]. - **Exclusion is real:** flat/minimal lacks clear affordances and is slower/less accurate for older adults [RN1]; minimalism risks "ambiguity or underspecification" with cultural/generational variance in reception [RN11].
- **Counter preserved:** minimalism genuinely aids clarity, message prioritisation and lower cognitive load for many [RN11, RN15]. So the grid-as-clarity vs grid-as-ideology tension holds.
- *Backing leans design-theory/semiotics + some usability empirics — flagged.*

## ✅ Critical-design cluster — CONFIRMED (canonical)
- **affirmative-vs-critical-design / speculative-design-for-debate:** Dunne & Raby's canon is foundational and heavily cited — *Speculative Everything* (≈1,500 cites) proposes "what if" design over prediction; *Hertzian Tales* names the "post-optimal object" and "aesthetics of user-unfriendliness." Pierce frames the shared "design resistance" technique across critical/speculative/adversarial/reflective design.
- **adversarial-design-and-agonism:** DiSalvo's *Adversarial Design* (≈475 cites) grounds design-as-contestation via agonism; recent CHI work designs *for* agonism (friction, refusal) as an alternative to consensus methods.
- **defamiliarisation-and-ambiguity:** Gaver's "Ambiguity as a Resource" (≈1,500 cites) directly argues ambiguity "encourages close personal engagement"; reflective-design and reflection-in-HCI traditions corroborate.

## ✅ passion-or-paycheck (Tokumitsu · Kim et al.) — CONFIRMED, genuinely two-sided
- **"Do what you love" can legitimise exploitation:** across 7 studies + a meta-analysis, people judge poor worker treatment (demeaning tasks, unpaid overtime) as **more legitimate when workers are presumed "passionate"**, via assumptions that the passionate would volunteer anyway and that the work is its own reward [PP-Ki]; the passion schema sorts and values labour in ways that reproduce race/class/gender inequality [PP-Wi, PP-Ra]; the "passion paradigm" shifts the locus of critique from institutions to the self [PP-De].

So "what is your passion being asked to pay for — the meaning, or a discount on what you're owed?" holds both poles.

## ✅ specialist-or-generalist (Ericsson · Güllich · Teodoridis) — CONFIRMED, two-sided (contingent on domain)

## ✅ the-average-and-the-edge (product-design · Daniels · Rose · Dreyfuss) — CONFIRMED, two-sided
- **No one fits the average:** anthropometric variation from the mean *grows* the further a population sits from it, so a single average misfits — personalised/ranged models are needed [AE-Hy]; face/body forms cluster into several sizing groups rather than one mean [AE-Ko]; the historical "average" was a male default, prompting average-*female* models for equitable safety design [AE-Jo]; averages also drift over time (secular change) [AE-Ca]; ergonomic fit depends on the spread, not the mean [AE-Al]. The canonical anchor — the USAF "average man" finding that almost no pilot fit the average across dimensions (Daniels, 1952) and Rose's *End of Average* (2016) — is Perplexity-verified.
- **Counter (held in-entry, design-economics not contested empirics):** designers cannot make infinite variants; a sensible set of sizes / adjustable range / shared conventions is unavoidable and often good, and full bespoke personalisation carries real cost. So "who does the average exclude — and would a range serve better?" holds both poles.

## ✅ emotion-or-the-functional (product-design · Norman) — CONFIRMED, two-sided (and contested)
- **The aesthetic-usability effect is real:** perceived beauty raises perceived usability even when actual usability is unchanged [AU-Tr (1420 cites)]; an appealing phone was rated more usable *and* completed tasks faster [AU-So]; high aesthetics *compensated* for poor usability, speeding completion [AU-Mo].

## ✅ who-gets-to-design (transdisciplinary · Ehn · Sanders & Stappers · Arnstein) — CONFIRMED, two-sided
- **Participation/co-design genuinely helps:** co-design teams generate concepts scoring higher in user benefit and novelty [WG-Tr]; research co-design benefits processes and outcomes [WG-Sl]; engaging users early is tightly bound to an application's success and user satisfaction [WG-Mi]; co-design can shift public services from expert-driven to user-driven [WG-Tr2].

## ✅ bootstrap-or-raise (entrepreneurship · Fried & Hansson / Ries) — CONFIRMED, two-sided
